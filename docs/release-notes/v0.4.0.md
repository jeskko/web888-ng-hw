**The channelizer bitstream with a zoom engine** (`websdr_pfb.bit`,
`docs/channelizer.md` §3.8 and §3.9). Signature `0x0E5D2010`, as since
the tuner (the list above still says `0x0E5D0010`).

* **Zoom spectra in the FPGA.** The server hands the FPGA jobs (a run of
  bins, an FFT length of 8 to 2048 points) and gets each job's power
  spectrum back on the power line's ring. A zoomed waterfall then costs
  the CPU a sum instead of its own FFTs. On the project's Web-888, 16
  listeners each with their own 480 kHz view run at 20 lines a second
  without an audio gap, the DSP pool at 60 % of a core; before, the pool
  was full at 99 % and shed lines.
* **Smaller.** The channelizer's memories are back in block RAM: before
  the engine the design fell from 83.8 % to 65.4 % of the LUTs. With
  the engine: 77.5 % LUT, 59 of 60 block RAMs, 71 of 80 DSP slices;
  timing met with the 0.4 ns guardband.
* **The driver** (`driver/`) has two new ioctls for the engine
  (`ZSDR_PFB_ZOOM`, `ZSDR_PFB_ZOOM_INFO`; `fpga/doc/register-map.md`).
  The engine sits idle until a driver enables it: an older driver and
  server run this bitstream as they ran v0.3.0's.
* **Tested on a Web-888 since 2026-11-11** with this repository's driver
  and web888d (the builds leading to v0.9.0): a remote unit with FT8
  skimmers on ten bands, load tests of 8 and 16 listeners with their own
  zoomed views. A zoomed view's spectrum from the engine and from the
  CPU's FFTs agree within 0.2 dB. That is one unit for some hours, not a
  long run. The bitstream here is CI's build of the same FPGA source as
  the one tested. In simulation the engine is bit-exact to its model
  (`fpga/scripts/zoom_model.py`).
* Not tested: a reset in the middle of a job, and switching the engine
  off during an FFT.
* HF and VHF bitstreams: as in v0.3.0, built from this tag and not run
  on hardware. v0.2.0's HF is the hardware-tested one; web888-ng-os keeps
  using it.

Use it on a unit you can't power-cycle only with the watchdog armed, as in
`docs/hardware-test-plan.md`.
